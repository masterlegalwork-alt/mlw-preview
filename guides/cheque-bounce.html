Cheque bounce (Section 138 NI Act)

Dishonour of a cheque for insufficient funds can be a criminal offence under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, but only if strict time limits are followed.

Cheque Bounce (NI Act)

Last reviewed 9 October 2026

Stage by stage

  1. Presentation and return

    The cheque must be presented within its validity period (currently three months from its date under RBI directions) and returned unpaid, for example for insufficient funds.

    Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

  2. Legal notice — 30 days

    The payee must send a written demand notice to the drawer within 30 days of receiving information of the return (s.138(b)).

    Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

  3. Time to pay — 15 days

    The drawer has 15 days from receipt of the notice to pay (s.138(c)). The offence is complete only if payment is not made in that time.

    Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

  4. Complaint — one month

    The complaint must be filed within one month of the cause of action (s.142(1)(b)); the court may condone delay for sufficient cause.

    Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

  5. Trial

    The court presumes the cheque was for a debt or liability (s.139); the accused may rebut this. Cases are to be tried summarily where possible (s.143), and the trial court may order interim compensation of up to 20% (s.143A).

    Rangappa v. Sri Mohan, AIR 2010 SC 1898, Supreme Court of India, decided 07.05.2010 · Meters and Instruments Pvt. Ltd. v. Kanchan Mehta, (2018) 1 SCC 560, Supreme Court of India, decided 05.10.2017 · Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

  6. Appeal

    In an appeal against conviction, the appellate court may order deposit of at least 20% of the fine or compensation (s.148).

    Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

  7. Settlement

    The offence is compoundable (s.147). The Supreme Court has encouraged settlement and early disposal.

    Meters and Instruments Pvt. Ltd. v. Kanchan Mehta, (2018) 1 SCC 560, Supreme Court of India, decided 05.10.2017 · Makwana Mangaldas Tulsidas v. State of Gujarat (order), SLP (Crl.) 5464/2016, order dated 05.03.2020, Supreme Court of India, decided 05.03.2020 · Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881

What to prepare

  • Keep the original cheque and the bank's return memo safely.
  • Calculate the 30-day notice deadline from the date you learned of the return.
  • Send the notice by a provable method (registered post / speed post, and email or WhatsApp where possible) and keep the receipts and tracking.
  • Keep the documents that show the debt (agreement, invoices, ledger, messages).

Documents to keep ready

  • Original cheque
  • Bank return memo
  • Copy of legal notice with postal receipts and tracking report
  • Proof of the underlying debt
  • For a company: board resolution or authority letter

Frequently asked questions

What if I miss the 30-day notice deadline?

A complaint under Section 138 is not maintainable without a valid notice within 30 days of information of the return. You may be able to present the cheque again within its validity and start afresh; take advice quickly.

Can the case be settled?

Yes. The offence is compoundable under s.147 of the NI Act, and the Supreme Court has encouraged compounding at an early stage.

Is interim compensation automatic?

No. Under s.143A the trial court may order interim compensation of up to 20%; the Supreme Court has held this power is discretionary.

I have been convicted. Must I deposit money to appeal?

The appellate court may direct a deposit of at least 20% of the fine or compensation under s.148; it can, in exceptional cases with reasons, decide otherwise.
