Arrest and bail

What happens from an FIR to bail, and the safeguards the law gives a person who is arrested. Since 1 July 2024 the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS) has replaced the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (CrPC) for new proceedings; older proceedings may still refer to CrPC sections.

Criminal & Bail

Last reviewed 9 October 2026

Stage by stage

  1. FIR or complaint

    Information about a cognizable offence is recorded as an FIR under s.173 BNSS (s.154 CrPC). The Supreme Court has held that registration of an FIR is mandatory when the information discloses a cognizable offence, with a preliminary inquiry permitted only in limited categories.

    Lalita Kumari v. Government of U.P., (2014) 2 SCC 1 (decided 12.11.2013) · Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023

  2. Notice or arrest

    For offences punishable with up to seven years, the police may issue a notice of appearance under s.35(3) BNSS (s.41A CrPC) instead of arresting. Arrest is not automatic and the reasons must be recorded.

    Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar, (2014) 8 SCC 273 (decided 02.07.2014) · Joginder Kumar v. State of U.P., (1994) 4 SCC 260 (decided 25.04.1994) · Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI, (2022) 10 SCC 51 (decided 11.07.2022) · Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023

  3. Rights on arrest

    The arrested person must be told the grounds of arrest (s.47 BNSS; s.50 CrPC), a relative or friend must be informed (s.48 BNSS; s.50A CrPC), and the person must be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours (s.58 BNSS; s.57 CrPC).

    D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal, (1997) 1 SCC 416 (decided 18.12.1996) · Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 · Constitution of India

  4. Remand

    The Magistrate decides whether custody is needed (s.187 BNSS; s.167 CrPC). If the investigation is not completed within 60 or 90 days (depending on the offence), the accused may apply for default bail.

    Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023

  5. Bail

    Bail in bailable offences is a matter of right (s.478 BNSS; s.436 CrPC). In non-bailable offences the court has discretion (s.480 BNSS; s.437 CrPC), and the Sessions Court and High Court have wider powers (s.483 BNSS; s.439 CrPC).

    Sanjay Chandra v. CBI, (2012) 1 SCC 40 (decided 23.11.2011) · Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI, (2022) 10 SCC 51 (decided 11.07.2022) · Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023

  6. Anticipatory bail

    A person who fears arrest for a non-bailable offence may apply to the Sessions Court or High Court (s.482 BNSS; s.438 CrPC). The Constitution Bench has held that such protection need not be limited to a fixed period as a rule.

    Sushila Aggarwal v. State (NCT of Delhi), AIR 2020 SC 831 (decided 29.01.2020) · Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023

  7. Quashing

    The High Court may quash an FIR or proceedings to prevent abuse of process (s.528 BNSS; s.482 CrPC). The Supreme Court has listed illustrative categories where this may be done.

    State of Haryana v. Bhajan Lal, AIR 1992 SC 604 (decided 21.11.1990) · Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023

What to prepare

  • Keep a copy of the FIR (it is available free of charge to the informant and often online through the State police portal).
  • Note the exact date and time of any notice, detention or arrest.
  • Collect documents that show your roots in the community: address proof, employment, family details.
  • Do not contact witnesses or the complainant about the case.

Documents to keep ready

  • FIR or complaint copy
  • Any notice received from the police
  • Arrest memo and remand orders
  • Identity and address proof
  • Earlier bail orders, if any
  • Medical records, if relevant

Frequently asked questions

Can the police arrest me without telling me why?

No. The grounds of arrest must be communicated to the arrested person (s.47 BNSS; s.50 CrPC), and a relative or friend must be informed (s.48 BNSS).

What is a notice of appearance?

For offences punishable with imprisonment of up to seven years, the police may require you to appear by notice instead of arresting you (s.35(3) BNSS; s.41A CrPC). Complying with the notice is important.

What is default bail?

If the police report is not filed within the period allowed under s.187 BNSS (s.167(2) CrPC), the accused becomes entitled to apply for bail on that ground.

Does anticipatory bail end when the charge-sheet is filed?

Not necessarily. The Supreme Court in Sushila Aggarwal held that it need not be limited to a fixed period as a rule; the court may impose conditions on the facts of the case.
